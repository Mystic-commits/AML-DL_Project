02 / Problem Statement 02 / 10

A fluent causal claim needs formal scrutiny.

Language models can generate plausible causal prose, but cannot by themselves guarantee acyclicity, estimate confounders, or answer interventional queries consistently.

Our Phase 1 architecture strictly isolates three distinct stages: reasoning entailment, lexical relation extraction with review, and Bayesian intervention queries.

04 / Classical ML Baselines 04 / 10

Transparent Classical Models

Vocabulary extracted strictly from training set (word unigrams + bigrams, single-letter variable support for A, B, C).

Candidate models: Majority Baseline, TF-IDF + Multinomial Naive Bayes, and TF-IDF + Balanced Logistic Regression.

Model selection strictly governed by Development Macro F1—test set is never touched during tuning.

06 / Shortcut Diagnostic 06 / 10

Exposing Spurious Dataset Cues

To determine if the classifier truly uses premise semantics, we trained a hypothesis-only control model.

The control reaches 0.653 Macro F1 without even observing the premise! This demonstrates that surface wording artifacts in synthetic templates explain a large portion of the score.

09 / Boundaries & Scope 09 / 10

What We Do & Do Not Claim

• No General NLP Understanding: Classical n-grams exploit lexical shortcuts rather than general d-separation logic.

• Decoupled Pipeline: Text graphs and synthetic Bayesian models are isolated educational modules; text does not automatically parameterize CPDs.

• Phase 1 Scope: Strictly classical ML and Bayesian networks. No deep learning, BERT, or LLM APIs are used.
